Community + Resources

I strive to create research and learning spaces where people can do their best work as their authentic selves. I value clear communication, kindness, and inclusive collaboration—because better science comes from broader participation and respectful teams. In my work, I aim to remove barriers to participation through mentoring, outreach, and service activities that support diversity, equity, inclusion, and justice.

What I Do

  • Teaching: I’ve been a Teaching Assistant at UMass Amherst for physiology and data science courses. I’ve also given guest lectures on local adaptation and phenotypic plasticity (including in ecophysiology and evolutionary ecology courses). My teaching has primarily been lecture-based so far, and I’m actively working toward incorporating more active-learning and inclusive practices as I gain experience.
  • Outreach: I enjoy building interactive, hands-on activities and explaining concepts in plain language. I’ve also created on-the-spot math support lessons to help students (grades K–12) work through topics they were stuck on. I’m especially motivated by helping people see the story behind science, how questions form, how uncertainty and iteration are part of the process, and how data become conclusions. I enjoy sharing parts of my scientific journey (and what I’ve learned navigating research and training) to make STEM feel more accessible and human.
  • Service: I’m still early in my graduate training and haven’t taken on many formal service roles yet. I’m interested in expanding this over time.
  • DEIJ Initiatives: I’ve participated in courses focused on social justice in STEM education and additional training on diversity and inclusion in STEM learning environments. I’m committed to continuing to learn and apply evidence-based practices in teaching, mentoring, and collaboration.

Mentoring

I believe good mentoring means meeting people where they are and helping them grow in directions that matter to them, whether that's research skills, confidence, professional development, or personal resilience in academia.

What mentees can expect from working with me
  • Clear expectations: We'll set goals together and talk through timelines, workload, and what support looks like.
  • Regular check-ins: We can check in on progress, troubleshoot challenges, and celebrate wins.
  • Feedback on your work: I provide constructive feedback on lab work, writing, presentations, and analyses.
  • Professional development (exploratory + supportive): I am early career but I'm happy to brainstorm, share what I have learned, connect you with others, and explore options together to find what works best for your goals.
  • Authentic support: I care about the whole person; well-being and long-term goals matter.

Reproducible Research Starter Kit

Recommended folder and repository structure:

project-name/
├── README.md
├── data/
│   ├── raw/
│   ├── processed/
│   └── metadata/
├── scripts/
│   ├── 01_data_import.R
│   ├── 02_clean_and_explore.R
│   └── 03_analysis.R
├── outputs/
│   ├── figures/
│   ├── tables/
│   └── models/
├── docs/
│   └── data_dictionary.md
└── .gitignore

README should include:

  • Project title and brief description
  • Authors and affiliations
  • Purpose, questions, and hypotheses
  • Data sources and collection methods
  • Folder structure overview
  • Steps to reproduce the analysis
  • Dependencies, software versions, and package versions
  • Contact information and license information

Data organization suggestions:

  • Keep raw data raw and never overwrite original files.
  • Use clean, descriptive file names with dates when relevant.
  • Store cleaned data in a separate processed folder.
  • Create a data dictionary that explains columns, units, and missing values.
  • Record collection metadata such as sampling date, site, person, and conditions.
  • Use numbered scripts to make workflow steps clear and reproducible.
Git & GitHub How-Tos
  • Main branch: Production-ready code only.
  • Develop branch: Working branch for testing new analyses.
  • Feature branches: For experiments and fixes.
  • Pull requests: Always use PRs to merge back to develop/main.
  • Commit messages: Be descriptive and specific.
R Workflows

I use a simple workflow structure so projects remain organized and reproducible. The key is to separate data, scripts, outputs, and notes clearly, and to keep the script itself readable and annotated.

# Analysis: [Brief description]
# Author: [Your name]
# Date: YYYY-MM-DD
# Notes: [Any important details]

# 1. Load packages
library(tidyverse)
library(ggplot2)

# 2. Source custom functions
source("scripts/shared_functions.R")

# 3. Load raw data
raw_data <- read.csv("data/raw/raw_data.csv")

# 4. Clean and format data
# 5. Explore patterns
# 6. Run analyses
# 7. Save outputs

Good practice: Use comments to label sections, save cleaned data as intermediate files, and keep the workflow in a logical order.

Plotting Standards & Figure Checklist
  • Axes are labeled clearly with units.
  • Legends are readable and positioned appropriately.
  • Color palette is accessible and colorblind-friendly.
  • Text size is large enough for presentation and publication.
  • Error bars or confidence intervals are documented.
  • Figure captions explain what is shown and why it matters.
  • Files are saved at adequate resolution and in reproducible formats.
  • Each figure is associated with the code and data used to generate it.
Field & Metadata Checklists

Field data collection checklist:

  • Include date, location, observer, and weather conditions.
  • Use consistent units and measurement protocols.
  • Record qualitative notes and unusual observations.
  • Pre-populate sample IDs to limit transcription errors.
  • Capture GPS coordinates or site identifiers when possible.

Metadata checklist:

  • Data collection dates and locations
  • Who collected and processed the data
  • Methods and reference to collection protocol
  • All column names, units, and data types
  • Missing values and how they are coded
  • Version number and file modification date
  • Known caveats, quality issues, and data limitations
